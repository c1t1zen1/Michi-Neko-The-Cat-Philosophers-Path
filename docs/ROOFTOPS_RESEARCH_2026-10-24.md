# Rooftops research — 2026-10-24

Notes behind the Roof Road (屋根道, `rooftops/`), the side-scrolling run
across the old city past the bridge. 26 searches and two full page reads.
"Read" = the page was fetched and summarised. "Snippet" = only the search
summary was available, so a claim from it goes no further than that summary.
Where sources disagree, both figures are given.

The design that uses all this is
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-24-rooftop-road-design.md`.

## The inner city: rakuchū and its "mouths"

| Fact | Use in the game | Source |
|------|-----------------|--------|
| In 1591 Hideyoshi ringed central Kyoto with the **Odoi** (御土居), an earthen bank about 22.5 km long. Land inside was *rakuchū* (洛中, "inside the capital"), land outside *rakugai* (洛外). Bamboo was planted on top (one source says for looks; another says a bamboo or wooden fence stood there). Height figures disagree (3–6 m, or 5 m on a 20 m base), and so do moat widths (6–18 m) | The rim past the valley bridge becomes an Odoi bank crowned with bamboo. Its thicket parting is the unlock, the same move as the shrine thicket | samurai-archives, tour-guide-japan (snippet) |
| The entrances through it are the "Seven Mouths of Kyoto" (京の七口, *Kyō no nanakuchi*). The count changed over time (six, seven, sometimes ten). **Awataguchi** (also called Sanjōguchi) stood **on the west bank of Sanjō Ōhashi** and joined the Tōkaidō and Nakasendō roads | The valley's bridge is the game's Sanjō Ōhashi: crossing it and passing the thicket is entering Awataguchi. The run is split into seven beats, one per "mouth" | shokunin.com (read) |
| Sanjō Ōhashi: Hideyoshi ordered it repaired in 1590. Onion-shaped **giboshi** finials sit on the railing posts, and one original is said to survive. A cut on a finial is *attributed* to the 1864 Ikedaya incident (one shop listing puts the mark on a different ornament) | Bronze giboshi caps on the bridge posts are the first hops in the run. The unlock counts the seven posts | Wikipedia, muza-chan (snippet) |

## Machiya: the surfaces the cat runs on

| Feature | What it is | Gameplay role | Source |
|---------|-----------|---------------|--------|
| *Kawara* roofs, *ichimonji* eaves | Dark-grey clay tiles; the *ichimonji* edge is a dead-straight, plain eave line that sets off the wave of the tiles | The main running surface: long flat ridges and eaves | nippon.com (read) |
| Uneven eaves | Eaves share one angle but sit at uneven heights, each overlapping its neighbour's to keep rain out of the gap | A built-in staircase: every house is a slightly different step up or down | nippon.com (read) |
| *Tsushi-nikai* (厨子二階) | A low second storey (servants' rooms and storage); the low roof was meant to stop fire jumping across the street | Sets the rhythm: low two-storey rows, about 4–5 m to the ridge in game scale | nippon.com (read) |
| *Mushiko-mado* (虫籠窓) | Small slatted windows set in plaster on the second floor (said to be a fire measure; one source) | Façade detail; slats are a toe-hold texture, not a platform | nippon.com (read) |
| *Kōshi* lattices | Ground-floor timber lattices whose pattern once named the trade (itoya, sakaya, komeya…) | Façade variety per street | nippon.com (read) |
| *Inuyarai* (犬矢来) | Curved bamboo guards at the foot of the wall against splashes, dirt and dogs | A curved ramp: the cat runs up it from the street (a climb-back) | nippon.com (read), japan-experience (snippet) |
| *Komayose* (駒寄せ) | Hardwood fence in front of the house, once a horse tether | A low step (≈1 m) | nippon.com (read) |
| *Battari-shōgi* | A bench that folds up against the lattice. The read source says the practice has died out | A 0.45 m step, down in the "open" position | nippon.com (read) |
| **Shōki** (鍾馗) figures | Small clay demon-queller figures on the lower eave. Several hundred remain in Kyoto. A neighbour often adds one when you do, and they're placed so they **don't stare at each other**. Kyoto is credited with making them from tile ceramic, as a homely alternative to temple *onigawara* | The collectible: seven Shōki to greet, one per mouth. Their gazes never meet, so each one looks *away* toward a hidden route | nippon.com (read), kyoto.travel, kotopotter (snippet) |
| *Monohoshidai* (物干し台) | A railed timber drying deck on the roof of urban machiya, because sun rarely reaches the tiny courtyards below | Laundry poles to tightrope, futons over the rail to bounce on | Jaanus/Columbia (snippet) |
| *Unagi no nedoko*, *tsuboniwa*, *kura* | "Eel's bed" plots: narrow frontage (tax by width is the usual story), deep lot, small courtyard gardens, a *kura* storehouse at the back | Kura are the tall walls of the level; tsuboniwa are green holes between roofs to hop over | kyoto-machiya-inn, Wikipedia (snippet) |
| *Roji* / *zushi* | Narrow passages; in Kyoto often cul-de-sacs cut into the over-large Heian blocks | The gaps between roofs, 1–2 m: the easy jumps | kyoto.travel (snippet) |
| Tile systems | *Hongawara* (flat pans + round covers), *sangawara* (the combined single tile, 1674), *noshigawara* ridge stacks, *onigawara* ridge-end tiles, *nokigawara* eave-end tiles | The visual vocabulary for roof builders; ridge stacks are a slightly higher "rail" | Jaanus, Wikipedia (snippet) |
| Bengara | Red-ochre iron-oxide wall finish of the Gion teahouses (Ichiriki Chaya on Shijō–Hanamikoji) | Colour code for the teahouse beat | muza-chan, Wikipedia (snippet) |

## The streets and districts (the beats)

| Place | What is true | Beat in the run | Source |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|--------|
| The grid | Heian-kyō (794) was laid out on a Chang'an-style grid, compared to a go board. Addresses use *agaru* (north) / *sagaru* (south); locals learn the east–west streets with the "Maru Take Ebisu" song | The run turns **right angles** at intersections; the camera swings round the corner | okamoto-kimono, tsunagu (snippet) |
| **Pontochō** | A narrow lantern-lit alley between the Kamo River and the Takase. Restaurants build summer platforms (*noryō-yuka*) over the Kamo, roughly May–September (*kawayuka*; in Kibune *kawadoko*, same kanji 川床). Plover (*chidori*) motifs appear on Pontochō lanterns; the plover's cry *chiyo* puns on 千代 "a thousand generations" | Beat 2: the high road over the roofs, or the low road along the yuka decks. The guide cat is named Chidori | magical-trip, gltjp, muza-chan, Jaanus (snippet) |
| **Takase River** | A canal Suminokura Ryōi built around 1611 to reach Fushimi. Flat-bottomed *takasebune* were hauled by hand. Only about **30 cm deep**. Cherry trees on the Kiyamachi bank | Beat 3: a drifting takasebune as the moving platform. A miss lands you in shallow water, so falling is safe here by geography | samurai-archives, Wikipedia (snippet) |
| **Nishiki Market** | A narrow covered market about 400 m long on Nishikikōji; the current canopy dates from 1993, the cobbles from 1984. The Teramachi and Shinkyōgoku arcades nearby (Shinkyōgoku built 1872) | Beat 4: a long canopy run (sprint, slide). The food moment: a fishmonger leaves a treat | Wikipedia (snippet) |
| **Kamo delta stepping stones** | At Demachiyanagi, concrete stepping stones (1992), some shaped like **turtles** and plovers | A hop line in the design doc. It echoes Larry the turtle in the valley | Atlas Obscura, Ritsumeikan (snippet) |
| **Gion Matsuri / Yoiyama** | Yoiyama evenings (14–16 and 21–23 July): streets close and the floats stand lantern-lit. *Hoko* floats are up to about 25 m to the pole tip and 12 t. Visitors **board some floats from the second floor of the neighbourhood's *kaisho* building** along a corridor. Float counts disagree between sources | Beat 6: cross the plank from a second floor onto a float and climb it. The side-scroll turns vertical | japan-guide, leafkyoto, timeout (snippet) |
| **Gozan no Okuribi** | 16 August, from 8 pm: Daimonji (大) first, then Myōhō, Funagata, Hidari-Daimonji and Toriigata. Each burns about 30 min. The Kamo banks between Sanjō and Imadegawa are a viewpoint. The middle of the order differs between sources | Beat 7: the finale. From the top of the float the five fires kindle one by one | Wikipedia, japan.travel (snippet) |
| **Gion Shirakawa** | A stone-paved canal lined with willows and cherries; the Tatsumi bridge and the small Tatsumi Daimyōjin shrine | Design-doc idea for a later east-side run | japan-experience, mai-ko (snippet) |
| **Nishijin** | The weaving district. The rhythmic clatter of looms; machiya as homes and workshops | Audio colour for a later run | kyoto.travel (snippet) |
| **Sannenzaka / Yasaka pagoda** | Stone-paved slopes; the five-storey Hōkan-ji pagoda (46 m in most sources, "over 30 m" in one) | A skyline silhouette now; a later "descending stair" run | gltjp, audiala (snippet) |
| **Kiyomizu stage** | "To jump from the stage at Kiyomizu" means to take the plunge. A temple diary records 234 Edo-period jumps (≈85% survived; one quote says "over 80%"). The practice was banned in 1872 | The design doc's "leap of faith" ending for the east-side run. It stays in the design doc only | temples.org, Nichibun (snippet) |
| Kyoto landscape rules | The 2007 landscape ordinances banned rooftop billboards and neon, cut height limits (31 m downtown, some areas down to 15 m) and protected views of the hills. A roof-pitch rule of 3:10–4.5:10 comes from one architect's page only | No neon and no signs on the roof tops; the hills stay in view. Roof slopes 17–24° (runnable) | Brumann/DIJ, artscape, architizer (snippet) |
| Funaoka Onsen | A 1923 bathhouse in Murasakino with a *karahafu* gate. No source mentioned a chimney | Not used. Sentō chimneys stay out until a source backs them | Wikipedia (snippet) |

## How high a cat jumps (for tuning)

Most sources give a horizontal leap of about **5–6 body lengths** and a vertical
jump of **1.2–2.4 m** from standing (estimates vary; no primary study turned up).
The game's numbers sit inside that range: 7.2 m/s take-off and −20 m/s² gravity
(×1.4 falling, ×1.7 after an early release) give a **1.30 m** apex.
Flat-ground reach is **3.0 m at the 4.5 m/s run** and **5.1 m at the 7.65 m/s sprint**
(floppycats, basepaws, pangovet; snippet).

## How other games bend a side-scroller

* **Klonoa** renders in 3D but keeps the player on a 2D path that curves, overlaps
  and branches. Players read it as 3D movement (Wikipedia, Nintendo Life; snippet).
* Implementation notes from engine forums: a spline or polyline path, positions
  derived from a 1D parameter plus height, forward-vector re-aimed through turns.
  The camera is the hard part (Unity, Godot and Babylon forums; snippet).
  The prototype takes the simplest robust form: a polyline with circular fillets,
  arc-length parameter `s`, and a camera that follows the path normal.
* **Stray**'s team prototyped the cat jumping onto air-con units first, and its
  animation was hand-keyed from mocap reference (Edge via PressReader, PC Gamer;
  snippet). No source confirmed how its jump input works, so the design
  claims nothing about it.

## Decisions for the prototype

1. **One collision plane, many depths.** Collision is 2D in (`s`, `y`). Each
   platform has its own depth offset, so a roof ridge sits behind its eave and the
   street sits in front. The cat's depth eases toward whatever it stands on, so a
   leap off an eave visibly drops *forward* into the street.
2. **No fail state by geography.** The street (and the 30 cm Takase) is always
   underneath. Every stretch of street has a climb-back within a short walk:
   barrel → eave, inuyarai ramp, lantern cap, komayose fence.
   `tools/rooftop_test.mjs` checks it.
3. **Game physics, unchanged.** The lab's jump, gravity, coyote time, buffer and
   variable height are copied from `src/player.js`, so a gap that works in the lab
   works in the game. The real `src/cat.js` is the runner.
4. **Progress, not a clock.** The sky moves from late afternoon to festival night
   as the cat moves along the route. Nothing is timed.
