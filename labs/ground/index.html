Every path begins
as a shortcut

Nobody draws the paths in a Kyoto garden first. Feet do: a cat crossing the moss to the water basin, the same way, every morning. This lab is a corner of Michi’s valley where that happens for real. The ground is earth, moss, gravel, white sand and stone that settle into each other by height. Where paws pass, the game’s own grass thins, the moss wears through and the earth packs down into ruts that fill when it rains. Then the gardener steps in with a broom, a rake and a ladle of water.

02

Anatomy of a lane

A shoulder is not a stripe. It is where the feet thin out.

  1. Meadow 野 untrodden: the game’s grass at full height.
  2. Shoulder 路肩 rokata: trodden now and then; stems flattened and straw-pale, the grass thinner and shorter.
  3. Rut 轍 wadachi: wheels and feet pack the masa-do down a few centimetres; the first puddles stand here.
  4. Median 中草: nothing treads the middle, so grass keeps a strip of it.
  5. Ditch 側溝 sokkō: the lowest line, so it fills first; tall wild grass on its banks.
  6. Under the eaves 雨落ち amaochi: in the corner’s north, pebbles where the roof drips, dry ground behind them.
