A background painter’s sky

Large, quiet shapes first. Cream crowns, cool violet bellies, and edges that catch the sun. A restrained paper texture breaks the digital smoothness; perspective and light keep the world believable.

A sky the valley can afford

The field and painted options are the everyday candidates. The volume study is the expensive reference. Eco keeps the volume at 12 view samples; Study allows 24. Costs shown beside recipes describe the algorithms, not promised frame times.

The shadow belongs to the cloud

Every new surface samples the same world-space weather map along the sun direction. Shade reduces direct sunlight, so the blue of the sky still lights the shadow. Low sunlight fades the projection before it stretches to infinity.

Where this could enter the game
  • src/sky.js would own the shared density field, wind offset and sun direction. Its current dome, sprites, moon and stars remain the comparison.
  • Patch direct sunlight on ground, roofs and grass with the same projection. Keep each material’s existing deformation and shadow hooks; see shadows.js.
  • Use the 256² field renderer first. The 64³ volume is a reference study, not a requirement for the valley.
  • Update PMREM only when the light and palette have drifted. This lab does not perform a cubemap bake every frame.
  • The original tea house, residence and village house in the observatory come from the game’s real builders. This lab does not alter the game or the summit.
Research, code & further reading

New primary-source reads, implementation choices and limits are recorded in the research notebook. These are independently written r160 studies of the techniques, with the official Three.js Sky imported as a named comparison. No cloud image packs or HDR downloads.
