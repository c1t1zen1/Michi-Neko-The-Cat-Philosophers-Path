    05 / NOTES FROM THE WORKBENCH
    Built for Michi’s Kyoto.
    The room remembers its materials.
    Kyōma mats use the Kyoto maker’s 191 × 95.5 cm module. Heri belongs on the long edges. The full-mat layouts are solved as tilings, with no four corners meeting. Wood has grain, paper has fibres, and bowls have a foot, an inside, and a hand-shaped rim.
    Light is the quiet furniture.
    Soft window fill, real lattice shadows, painted branch silhouettes, and warm andon light create the mood. Shoji diffusion is an artistic approximation; the paper control is not a physical transmission measurement.
    A house with good manners.
    The formal tea room stays sparse. Everyday rooms get low dining tables; the ceremonial room gets a brazier and utensils. The tokonoma remains a display alcove. Michi’s knockable cups live on furniture, and always come back in one piece.
    Ready for a game review.
    This is a standalone lab. Its reusable room kit is organised into floors, frame, screens, alcove, furniture, and atmosphere. The game’s cat comes through the shared Labs bridge; no valley layout or summit code changes.
