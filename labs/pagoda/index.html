    04

    What reads at a kilometre.

    Findings from the platform, the records and the painters.

    1. Five dark lines. The bracket zone and soffit under each roof are the darkest values in the tower. They survive to about two pixels a storey, after the brackets, rafters and bells have gone. Keep them dark in every tier: that is the far tier’s whole job.
    2. The needle. The sōrin is a quarter to a third of the height (Hōryū-ji 1 : 3.4; Daigo-ji 13 m of 38). The game’s finial is about a fifth. At range the spire, not the roofs, is what says “pagoda” and not “tall house”.
    3. Taper sets the silhouette. Top ÷ first storey runs from 0.51 (Hōryū-ji: a cone) to about 0.8 (Ninna-ji: a column with fins). Because the eaves barely shrink while the body does, the eave-to-body ratio climbs from 2.2 to 3.0, and the upper roofs look wide.
    4. The lift is small, so paint it bigger. Measured corner rise is only 1–5% of the eave (Yamahata: 0.0063–0.048); on a 14 m eave that is half a metre, sub-pixel beyond ~200 m. Background painters push what must read; the valley preset doubles it.
    5. Distance is a colour. Air removes blue first, so timber warms into violet-grey and the veil takes it, bent gold toward the sun. Value steps between the tower, the town and the three ridges do the depth. Line work should thin out with distance, too.
    6. Bells are a sound, not a shape. Twenty fūtaku are a few pixels each beyond a hundred metres. In the valley they belong in the audio: a chime in the wind, panned toward the tower.
    05

    Back to the valley.

    How this could replace buildDistantPagoda(). A lab first; nothing here changes the game.

    • The valley preset at the mid tier. From the bridge (57 m) the tower is about 300 px tall: mid tier (shader tiles, a bracket shelf, a painted soffit, a band kōran). Same 19.4 m height and dais footprint as today’s, and the forest sightline in buildEdgeForest() still applies.
    • Fewer objects, not fewer triangles. Today’s tower is 93 separate meshes. Each builder output here is a handful of merged and instanced meshes per storey. Registering the materials in MAT would let _mergeStatics() fold most of them.
    • The pixel-budget rule. Choose tiers by height on screen, so the same rule works from the bridge and from the hills, and crossfade through the Bayer screen door rather than blending.
    • Physical light underneath, paint on top. The air patch matches the game’s haze to distance and sun, and the background-art finish is a candidate for a global post pass, subject to the governor’s budget on real devices.
    • Fūtaku in the soundscape. The bell synth in sound.js could ring with the game’s wind, quietly, from the tower’s direction.
    • Still to do before promotion: device benchmarks, a seeded review sheet in tools/, material registration, and a check against the summit’s own Kyoto pagoda, which belongs to the summit effort.
