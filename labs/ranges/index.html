05 / THE AIR

Five veils, or one warm one.

The same ridge five times, at 46 m, 110 m, 200 m, 330 m and 480 m — the veil is exponential, so the far plane carries far more sky than the near one. Flip to reverse and the lowest sun warms the farthest ridge instead: painters documented that too.

09 / NOTES FROM THE WORKBENCH

Built for Michi’s Kyoto.

The basin is the composition.

Kyoto is walled on three sides — Higashiyama east, Kitayama and Atago north-west, Arashiyama west — and opens south. The game’s terrainSector is exactly that mask; the lab builds each side as its own layer so the opening can breathe.

The veil is exponential, not linear.

Gurney’s wedding veils: each farther plane carries more sky light, geometrically. Linear fades flatten the far range into the sky; 1 − e^(−dβ) keeps a distant ridge readable at 300 m and lets a low sun light the whole veil at once.

Cheap trees spend their budget at the edge.

The four wall techniques cost between one draw and nine, and between 300 and 60,000 triangles. What the player reads is silhouette and motion — the impostor atlas spends its pixels exactly there.

Kasumi is compositing by hand.

Mist bands divide planes in every Yamato-e screen worth its gold leaf. Ours are scrolling textures on flat cards — the same compositing, done by the depth buffer instead of a painter’s hand.
